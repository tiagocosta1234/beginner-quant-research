# Pre-registration BRK-ROUND (breakout day, round 27; written 2026-10-15 13:46 local, local clock, before any code or result)
Question: do breakouts THROUGH ROUND NUMBERS run further than other breakouts? Published basis: Osler (2003, Journal of Finance, "Currency orders and exchange rate dynamics") found stop-loss orders clustered just beyond round numbers in a bank's FX order book, so crossings of round numbers should accelerate ("price cascades"), while take-profit orders at round numbers stop moves before them.
Round step per market and day (from the previous server day's mid close P): the 1-2-5 x 10^k value nearest to 0.5% of P (EURUSD 1.08 -> 0.005 = the 00 / 50 levels; USDJPY 150 -> 0.5; gold 4,000 -> 20; Nasdaq 20,000 -> 100; BTC 100,000 -> 500).
Event (long; shorts mirrored): the first M1 bar whose ask high reaches the first round level ABOVE the previous 24 hours' ask high (a fresh level, untouched for 1,440 minutes); a buy stop order at that level fills at max(level, ask open). Stop = 0.5 step below the entry; R = 0.5 step (>= 2 spreads required).
- **K1:** hold 60 minutes (or the stop).
- **K2:** hold 4 hours (or the stop).
Exits capped at 22:55 server of the entry day; no entry in server hours 23 / 0; one trade at a time per market and cell. 46 markets.
Controls: (a) **non-round level**: the same rule with levels shifted by 0.37 step (a fresh 24-hour breakout through an arbitrary level at the same spacing) - isolates the round-number effect; (b) random entry (random side, same minute, same R and exit).
Gates (DEV 2022-24, VAL 2025, 2026 non-clean): 1. DEV mean >= +0.05 R and t >= 2.24 (daily NW lag 1; Bonferroni 2). 2. VAL mean > 0 and t >= 1.65. 3. Beats the non-round control by >= 0.05 R in DEV and in VAL.
Honest prior: about 8% (published microstructure basis, but the stop clusters are in interbank FX order books of the 1990s, not CFDs now).
Correction before the run (13:45, dry-run only, no P&L seen): the rule "nearest 1-2-5 value to 0.5% of P" is measured as the nearest in log ratio; for USDJPY at 150 that is 1.0 (the big figures), not 0.5 as the example above said. The rule is unchanged; the examples are: EURUSD 0.005, USDJPY 1.0, gold 20, Nasdaq 100, BTC 500, WTI 0.5.
Amendment B1 (13:47, AFTER the first run - a bug fix, not a tuning; both runs are reported): the first run let a buy stop that filled inside a minute be stopped out by that minute's low and filled at that minute's OPEN, both of which can come before the fill (BTCUSD: 33% of trades below -1.05 R, worst -16 R). Fixed in `brk_hours.hold_fill`: the fill minute stops the trade only if it closes beyond the stop (exit at the stop); the regular check starts at the next minute. The rules of the test are unchanged.
