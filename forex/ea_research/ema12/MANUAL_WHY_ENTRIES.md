## Side thread, 20 Oct evening: "put limit orders on the prices, with the spread, so we get in and out on the prices we wanted" (the trader)
Mechanics: the chart shows the bid. A buy fills on the ASK, a sell on the BID; a long pays the spread at entry, a short at exit (its target / stop fill on the ask). A limit order fixes the price, not the fill: a buy limit at the chart (bid) price fills only when the ask falls to it, i.e. when price has moved against you by the spread (0.48 now, live ticks 13-20 Oct); a sell limit at the current bid fills at once. So limit orders turn the spread into a fill probability, they do not remove it.
Check on his 49 trades with a stop (`manual_limit_entries.py`, `MANUAL_LIMIT_ENTRIES_OUTPUT.txt`, MT5 broker bid / ask, descriptive, n 24 buys): market entry mean R +0.150 (buys +0.054, sells +0.242); buy limits at the chart price, 5 minutes: 19 of 24 fill (79%), their R rises from -0.055 to +0.001 (the spread saved is about 0.06 R), but the 5 unfilled buys were all winners (mean +0.47 R); all 49 sum R +7.34 at market against +6.07 with the buy limits (mean +0.150 -> +0.138). Same pattern as the mechanical Q model (EMA1000_NOCOST: fill 81%, still negative).
What is left of the idea: limit orders avoid slippage and spike fills; a short's target must be set one spread inside the chart level (smaller target); the cost is a fact of the quote, so the size of the target relative to 0.48 matters (targets under about 4 points are marginal).
