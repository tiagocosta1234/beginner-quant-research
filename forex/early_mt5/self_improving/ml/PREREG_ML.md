# Pre-registration: machine learning on the raw bars (written 2026-10-13, before any ML code ran)

the trader: "go ahead with the regime layer, then machine learning on the raw bars." The regime layer is closed (REGIME_RESULT.md, 0 survivors). This is the second part.

## What the models see and predict
Raw input, no hand-made indicators: for every decision (a 15-minute bar close) the last 48 bars of the market (12 hours): open, high, low, close of the BID as log(price / current close) divided by the bar volatility (causal std of bar returns over 384 bars), plus two M1-derived numbers per bar (the bar's 1-minute realised variance over the volatility squared, and its mean spread over its recent median); plus hour and weekday (sine and cosine), and the log of the current bar volatility over its 2,000-bar median. 293 numbers. Class-pooled (one model per class: idx 6, fx 28, cmd 4, crypto 8 markets). Target: the gross bid-to-bid return from the next bar's open to the close H bars later, over (volatility x sqrt(H)), winsorised at +/-5. H in {4, 16, 96} bars (1 hour, 4 hours, 1 day). 12 cells (4 classes x 3 horizons).
Model: gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost, histogram, squared error, depth 5, learning rate 0.05, up to 400 trees with early stopping, subsample 0.7, column sample 0.5, min child weight 200, L2 10), fixed in advance. A convolutional network on the same windows is NOT part of the gate: it is run later, once, only for cells where the trees show a signal, and reported as exploratory.

## Windows and protocol (nothing is tuned on VAL)
Train on window A (2022-01..2023-06), rows on the H = 4 bar grid (position in the market's window divisible by 4); labels whose exit would fall after the window end are dropped. Window B (2023-07..2024-12) is used for the early stopping and for choosing the signal threshold. VAL (2025) is looked at ONCE per cell. 2026 is never loaded.
Signal and trades: long if the prediction >= tau, short if <= -tau, entries on the grid of spacing H (so holds never overlap inside a market), at the next bar's ask / bid, exit at the bar close H bars later with the real bid / ask, plus the flat conservative carry of the earlier tests; tau is the quantile of |prediction| on B (among 50, 70, 80, 90, 95%) that maximises the net mean on B with at least 100 trades.
Scores on VAL: information coefficient (Spearman correlation of prediction and target on all VAL rows), net mean bp per trade, and the gross excess over random timing on the same grid (cluster-robust weekly z, Student t one-sided p).
Cell passes (S_ML) only if on VAL: >= 100 trades, >= 20 weekly clusters, net mean >= +3 bp, and the excess p < 0.05 / 12 (Bonferroni over the 12 cells). A pass is then judged in the MT5 broker environment (stage E of PREREG_V5_PROP.md) before anything else.

## Calibration (before the real run)
The identical pipeline (windows, training, threshold choice, VAL) on three never-seen placebo worlds (seeds 51-53): every 15-minute bar of a class shuffled by one common permutation (as in PREREG_V6_M1.md), so no relation between a window and the future exists by construction. Gate: 0 cells pass in every world, and the largest cell IC and excess z of the worlds are reported as the noise ceiling to compare with the real cells. If the gate fails it is reported and the real run does not start.

## Expectations written now
Trees on 12 hours of raw bars at 15-minute resolution are a standard attempt and the literature finds very small out-of-sample information coefficients (below 0.01-0.02) that costs usually absorb. The most likely outcome is no passing cell. The value is a calibrated answer to 'does a flexible learner find anything in the raw bars that the rule search could not', with the placebo ceiling next to every real number.

Calibration gate: PASSED. Placebo worlds 51, 52, 53: 0 of 12 cells pass in every world; noise ceiling over the 36 placebo cells: max VAL information coefficient +0.0237, max VAL excess z 2.81 (the Bonferroni bar is p < 0.0042, i.e. z about 2.7 at 50 clusters). Real run follows, same code.
